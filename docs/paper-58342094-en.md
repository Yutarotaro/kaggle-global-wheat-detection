# Evaluating a Two-Detector Ensemble for Wheat Head Detection Across Acquisition Sources

Technical paper draft · Global Wheat Detection · Kaggle submission `58342094` · 14 November 2026

[PDF version (A4, two columns)](figures/paper-58342094-en.pdf) · [日本語版](paper-58342094.md)

## Abstract

Wheat head detection in field images is sensitive to acquisition conditions and head density. We split the Kaggle Global Wheat Detection training images by acquisition source and trained MobileNetV3-Large Faster R-CNN and EfficientDet-D2 independently. We combined their predictions using Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF) and evaluated the ensemble on 636 images from two sources excluded from training. At an output confidence threshold of 0.40, WBF achieved a validation score of 0.580485, compared with 0.522629 for Faster R-CNN and 0.544813 for EfficientDet-D2. Relative to EfficientDet-D2, WBF reduced false and duplicate detections at IoU 0.50 while increasing missed detections. The late Kaggle submission scored 0.6512 on the Public leaderboard and 0.5466 on the Private leaderboard. Performance still varied markedly across acquisition sources, and recall for small heads remained low.

## 1 Introduction

Detecting individual wheat heads in field images supports estimates of head count and density. Occlusion, background, imaging equipment, and growth stage vary across acquisitions. The Global Wheat Head Detection (GWHD) dataset provides images and bounding-box annotations from multiple institutions and fields, allowing detectors to be evaluated under varied conditions. [1](#references)

We combine a two-stage detector that classifies and refines region proposals with a one-stage detector that predicts classes and box offsets directly from anchors on feature maps. We examine whether fusing their predictions improves the score on the same images at a common output threshold, and how fusion affects false and missed detections. [3-7](#references)

## 2 Data and evaluation

### 2.1 Acquisition-source split

We used the images and annotations in the Kaggle competition's `train.csv`. Forty-nine images lacked annotations and source metadata in the CSV and were excluded. We split the remaining images by acquisition source with seed 42 and kept exact image duplicates within the same split. The 735-image local holdout was reserved for final confirmation and remained unscored at the time of this report.

| Split | Images | Acquisition sources |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Training | 2,002 | `arvalis_1` (1,055), `ethz_1` (747), `usask_1` (200) |
| Exploratory validation | 636 | `arvalis_2` (204), `rres_1` (432) |
| Local holdout | 735 | Reserved by acquisition source |

The median number of annotated heads per image was 50 in training and 37 in validation. Fewer than 25 heads were annotated in 101 training images (5.0%) and 173 validation images (27.2%). In `arvalis_2`, 156 images (76.5%) fell in this range. [Data profile](data-profile.md)

### 2.2 Evaluation metric

For each image and intersection-over-union (IoU) threshold, we sorted predictions by descending confidence and matched each prediction to the unmatched ground-truth box with the highest IoU. A match counted as a true positive when its IoU exceeded the threshold. Each ground-truth box could be matched once. We averaged `TP / (TP + FP + FN)` over images and six IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.75 in increments of 0.05. Duplicate detections count as false positives in this metric. In the error analysis, we counted them separately when an unmatched prediction exceeded the IoU threshold against an already matched ground-truth box. We defined small heads as those with a ground-truth box covering less than 0.2% of the image and measured their recall at IoU 0.50. Public and Private scores are the values returned by Kaggle. ## 3 Proposed method

![Two detector branches share an RGB input. Faster R-CNN uses MobileNetV3-Large, FPN, RPN, and RoIAlign with classification and box regression heads. EfficientDet-D2 uses EfficientNet-B2, BiFPN, and dense classification and regression heads. Both branches return boxes in the original image coordinates for weighted boxes fusion.](figures/wheat-58342094-architecture.svg)

**Figure 1. Architecture and inference path.** Solid arrows carry images, features, region proposals, or predictions. The dashed arrow supplies FPN features for the proposed regions to RoIAlign.

### 3.1 MobileNetV3-Large Faster R-CNN

Branch A uses TorchVision's `fasterrcnn_mobilenet_v3_large_fpn`. MobileNetV3-Large extracts features at multiple resolutions, which the feature pyramid network (FPN) combines. The region proposal network (RPN) proposes candidate regions. RoIAlign pools features for each region from the FPN, and the classification and regression heads predict a wheat-head score and a refined box. We initialized the detector with COCO-pretrained weights, replaced its final predictor with a two-class head for wheat heads and background, and set the input size to 640 px. [3-5](#references)

### 3.2 EfficientDet-D2

Branch B uses `tf_efficientdet_d2`. The image is resized to 768 by 768 pixels. A bidirectional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) fuses features from the EfficientNet-B2 backbone, and dense classification and regression heads predict a score and box for each anchor. We initialized the detector from pretrained weights and fine-tuned it for one foreground class. [6](#references)

### 3.3 Training and box fusion

We fine-tuned both detectors independently on the same 2,002 images using a Kaggle Tesla T4, seed 42, batch size 4, AdamW with a learning rate of 0.0001, and horizontal flips with probability 0.50. We used the final checkpoints after 12 epochs for Faster R-CNN and 6 epochs for EfficientDet-D2. The submitted models used the Kaggle training split.

After non-maximum suppression (NMS), each branch passes detections with confidence of at least 0.30 to WBF. We map boxes to the original image and normalize coordinates by image width and height. WBF gives each model weight 0.5 and groups boxes whose IoU with the current fused box exceeds 0.55. A box contributes to the fused coordinates in proportion to the product of its confidence and its model weight. We use the `box_and_model_avg` confidence rule from `ensemble-boxes 1.0.9`. With equal model weights, a cluster with one box from each detector receives the mean of their scores. A cluster with a single box receives half its original score. We convert the fused boxes back to image coordinates, retain those with confidence of at least 0.40, and export them in the submission format. We selected the output threshold on the exploratory validation split. [7](#references) · [Submission configuration](../configs/submission-d2-mbv3-wbf040.json)

## 4 Results

### 4.1 Validation across acquisition sources

At the common output confidence threshold of 0.40, WBF outscored EfficientDet-D2 by 0.035672 on the 636 validation images and exceeded the best individual detector on both acquisition sources. Its score was 0.675937 on `rres_1` and 0.378352 on `arvalis_2`.

| Method | Overall | `rres_1` (432) | `arvalis_2` (204) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MobileNetV3-Large Faster R-CNN | 0.522629 | 0.611282 | 0.334894 |
| EfficientDet-D2 | 0.544813 | 0.650899 | 0.320160 |
| **Two-detector WBF** | **0.580485** | **0.675937** | **0.378352** |

At IoU 0.50, WBF reduced false detections excluding duplicates from 2,619 to 1,688 and duplicates from 108 to 48 relative to EfficientDet-D2. Missed heads increased from 4,663 to 5,094. Recall on the 957 small heads fell from 9.82% to 5.96%.

**Table 3. Detection errors at IoU 0.50.**

| Method | False positives | Duplicates | Misses | Small-head recall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MobileNetV3-Large Faster R-CNN | 3,151 | 174 | 4,901 | 64 / 957 (6.69%) |
| EfficientDet-D2 | 2,619 | 108 | 4,663 | 94 / 957 (9.82%) |
| Two-detector WBF | 1,688 | 48 | 5,094 | 57 / 957 (5.96%) |

## 5 Discussion and limitations

At a common output threshold, fusion reduced false and duplicate detections while increasing misses. This pattern is consistent with downweighting boxes supported by just one detector. The low recall on small heads motivates studying how to handle high-confidence detections from only one model. The raw detector scores were not calibrated before fusion.

Head counts differed between the training and validation sets, and performance varied across the two validation sources. This evaluation cannot isolate the effects of head density, background, imaging equipment, or other acquisition differences. The score of 0.580485 is exploratory because we selected the confidence threshold on the same split used to report it. Independent local holdout evaluation and runs with multiple seeds remain to be done. The trial using additional images from Mons falls outside this submission snapshot.

The Private score increased by 0.1017 over our initial submission. This comparison combines changes in training duration, model composition, and confidence threshold. The winning 2020 entry also used multiple models, external data, test-time augmentation, and pseudo-labeling. Its reported Private score of 0.6897 was 0.1431 higher than ours. [8-9](#references)
